Dream Jumper

A liminal horror walking sim where every dream follows its own rules.

The concept

A first-person liminal horror walking sim. The player moves through three recurring dreams, in a random order each run, and each dream ends with the same chase and a ringing alarm clock. Every dream belongs to someone the protagonist loves, and the level has to show who.

My role
  • Level design. I designed the levels of the first three dreams, from the layout to the implementation in the engine, such as Poolrooms, The Playground and Hubert's Office
  • Game design. I wrote the game design document and defined the rules of each dream.
  • UI. I designed the user interface, from the wireframes to the screens in the game.
  • Production. I directed the production of the project’s other components.
    • Level design: narrative through space

    The game’s narrative philosophy lives in the levels as much as in the writing. A dream has to express its owner, so the level itself carries the story:

  • Layout: the shape of the space.
  • Light and color: the tones of each dream.
  • Entities: who and what lives there.
  • Puzzles and props: what the player does and finds.

    • Example: the pool level. It is intricate, and in places anxious, because of creatures that react to sound, and its tones sit in aquamarine. Not every level works this way: some include contemplative, artistic stretches that give the player room to breathe between dreams.

    Design highlights
  • Enemies that hunt by sound. They are blind. Two concentric zones define their hearing: an inner zone where they always notice you, and an outer one where crouching keeps you hidden.
  • Death as a mechanic. When you die, the dream shows its true face: the same scene, incomplete, with missing elements, new parkour routes and more aggressive enemies.
  • Diegetic puzzles. Systems live in the world, for example dosing a specific pool with chlorine from a lever to unlock a way through.
    • UI

    The wireframes cover the whole flow: a pause menu, a “You Faded Away” death screen, a “You’ve Made It!” end-of-demo screen, and an in-game HUD with a timer, a health value, an equipped-item slot, a dialogue box and an eye-shaped awareness arc. The arc is part of the design too: it ties what the player knows to how the dream plays.

    Challenges
  • Fitting three complete dreams into a 30-minute demo.
  • Making sound-based stealth readable without UI prompts.
  • Keeping level, UI and the other components aligned while directing their production.
